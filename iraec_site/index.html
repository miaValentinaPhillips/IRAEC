Your starting point in aerospace

No experience? No problem.

You don't need prior rocketry or aerospace experience to join IRAEC. Whether you're new to engineering or have already built and launched rockets, there's a place for you on our team. We learn together, ask questions, and build real skills through hands-on projects.

Every fall, we host weekly educational lectures using topics from Rocket Propulsion Elements. These sessions introduce students to propulsion fundamentals and the engineering principles behind rocket flight, helping new members develop the background to contribute confidently to our projects.

Flagship project

Project Lifeline

IREC 2027 • 10K COTS

Our first IREC campaign. A foundation for what comes next.

Project Lifeline marks IRAEC's first planned entry into the International Rocket Engineering Competition. For our team, the mission is about more than reaching an altitude target: it is about creating the technical processes, mentorship network, and institutional knowledge that future GVSU rocketry teams can build upon.

Our single-stage rocket program integrates airframe design, motor selection, avionics, recovery, and a communications-focused payload. Students take ownership of design reviews, documentation, testing, and launch readiness.

Project Lifeline / Mission Payload

The historical inspiration behind Project Lifeline

Our experimental payload, Project Lifeline, is inspired by the 126th Infantry Regiment of the Michigan National Guard and its experiences at Huggins’ Roadblock during World War II, where unreliable communications made it difficult for isolated soldiers to request assistance.

Project Lifeline will investigate the reliability and range of VHF and UHF radio communications throughout the rocket’s ascent. The payload will continuously transmit a Morse code pattern containing its assigned callsign, which multiple ground stations at varying horizontal distances will attempt to receive.

By analyzing signal strength, reception quality, and interference at different altitudes, our team hopes to better understand the factors affecting long-distance radio communication. The project connects a historical challenge faced by Michigan soldiers to a modern student-led aerospace communications experiment.

As a first-year IREC team, our goal is not only to successfully complete our first competition flight but also to establish a strong foundation for future GVSU rocketry teams through hands-on engineering, interdisciplinary collaboration, and meaningful experimental research.

Payload design, testing procedures, frequencies, and experimental implementation remain subject to engineering review and competition requirements.
